Dignity Resolution & Budget Review

November 19, 2026 | Troy City Council Finance Meeting

← Back ▶ Watch on YouTube Finance Agenda Finance Minutes Committee Budget Reports

No minutes were posted for the budget review session that followed the Finance Meeting, which took no votes. That part of the summary comes from the meeting recording.

Synopsis

The administration asked the council to adopt a resolution affirming everyone's right to dignity in local government. The council passed it 7-0 after amending it, on a motion by Council Member Shikole Struber, to cover the administration too and to welcome the mayor, deputy mayor, and corporation counsel to attend public meetings in chambers. The council also approved two union agreements, an Ethics Commission appointment, and a state-funded tree contract for Prospect Park, all 7-0.

On the third quarter financial report, Council Member Greg Campbell-Cohen said state figures show the city is unlikely to hit its $22 million sales tax budget, and Council Member Jona Favreau said the council's questions had gone without solid answers. In the budget review session that followed, the administration objected to the council's proposed amendments, including raises for the clerk's office and filling evening and weekend parking enforcement.

Resolution 106: The Dignity Resolution, Amended

Finance Committee, chaired by Council President Sue Steele. Called to order at 6:00 p.m. Present: Council Members Phil DiLorenzo, Struber, Kathleen Spain-McLaren, Noreen McKee, Favreau, and Campbell-Cohen, and Council President Steele. In attendance: Mayor Carmella Mantello, Deputy Mayor Seamus Donnelly, Corporation Counsel Richard Morrissey, Comptroller Michael McNeff, Head of Finance and Personnel Gabrielle Mahoney, Commissioner of General Services Joseph Mazzariello, and Communications Director Alex Horton. Adjourned at 7:34 p.m.

Struber moved the amendment, seconded by Favreau. It adds "the Administration" to the list of officials and employees the resolution covers, and "all" before "City officials." It adds clauses saying dignity "includes actively listening to what they are saying preferably from the same room," and that empathy builds trust between residents and government. And it adds a final resolve that the council "will continue to welcome the Mayor, Deputy Mayor, and Corporation Counsel to join the Council and the residents in chambers during all public meetings."

Spain-McLaren, who works with young people every day, said adults have to model these values, and thanked resident Greg Lyons for the examples Lyons raised in the public forum. "I hope that it's being taken seriously by all those involved," she said. Steele said dignity "requires all parties," and that she would do her best to keep the chamber respectful. The amendment passed 7-0, and the amended resolution passed 7-0.

The budget review session ended on the same theme. As the administration left, Steele asked about scheduling a council tour of the new City Hall and noted that Channel 13 had already been given one. "All seven of you were invited and all seven of you declined to come," Donnelly said. "That is not true, Seamus," Steele said, adding that she'd had a doctor's appointment. "What happened to the mutual respect and dignity?"

Third Quarter Report: Questions Without Answers

McNeff reported that General Fund revenues are at about 74 percent of budget through September and the city has collected about 95 percent of the property tax levy. Sales tax is up $687,000, about 5.5 percent, over last year, though that covers only two of the county's four quarterly payments. About $13 million of the $22 million budget has come in, and McNeff said the city is "cautiously optimistic" it will get there.

Campbell-Cohen questioned that. According to the state Comptroller's office, he said, Rensselaer County collected $102.76 million in sales tax from January through September, and to hit the city's budgeted number the county would need about $47 million more from October through December. "We're not hitting that number." McNeff said the city still has two county payments to receive and hadn't seen the state report. Campbell-Cohen said he would send it over.

Other revenue questions went largely unanswered on the spot. Licenses and permits are down about 28 percent. State aid is well under budget until the state's Aid and Incentives for Municipalities (AIM) payment arrives in December. Favreau asked about the city's share of cannabis sales tax, which she had raised by email earlier in the year; McNeff said about $26,000 has posted, but confirming it would require pulling cash receipts by hand. Favreau said her own math from the General Services hearing shows parking and other transportation revenue coming in at least $400,000 short. "I'm feeling a little frustrated because all of the questions we've asked, you've had no solid answers for," she said.

Mayor Mantello said a lot of money arrives in the fourth quarter and that the city finished last year with a $1.2 million surplus. "If we were concerned, we would tell you." The 2025 annual financial report the comptroller presented on May 7 showed a General Fund surplus of $453,000. The mayor also blamed a 1980s accounting system that runs on paper requisitions and payroll entered twice by hand, and said the new Tyler Technologies system will fix it.

When Steele cut in to keep the meeting on track, the mayor said, "We get the laughter from the audience. Come on, Council President. This is a serious matter." Spain-McLaren said most council members and residents aren't financial experts, and many of the council's questions come from the public. "We shouldn't be feeling like we're getting our hand slapped for asking questions that are valid."

Struber noted the third quarter report shows $1.1 million budgeted for judgments and claims in 2026, while the proposed 2027 budget lists $100,000 for the same year. Donnelly said the higher number reflects one large settlement this year, and $100,000 is the normal level. Struber suggested bringing department heads to future quarterly reports, since many questions are theirs to answer, and McNeff agreed. Water sales look low only because a few billing cycles shifted two days into October, McNeff said; Donnelly added that the city is now billing outside communities that buy Troy water for their share of the transmission main project, money it hadn't been collecting.

Steele closed the report: "It is the duty of the council to monitor the finances of the city, and so we will continue to ask the questions going forward, and do it in an open environment."

Ethics Commission, Prospect Park Trees, and Union Agreements

Resolution 105 appoints Sarah, an attorney, to the city's Ethics Commission (her last name wasn't clear on the recording). Spain-McLaren said she looks forward to the commission finally holding meetings, which is "long overdue." Campbell-Cohen, the co-sponsor, thanked Sarah for volunteering.

Resolution 107 hires Richard Sears Tree Experts and Allmark Tree Services for pruning, removal, and planting in Prospect Park, paid by a state forestry grant of about $384,000. Favreau said the 2019 Community Forest Management Plan the resolution cites is still marked as a draft on the city website, and asked whether it was ever adopted. Morrissey didn't know and said the planning department has had a lot of turnover. Favreau said she wants to know how many trees will be cut versus planted.

Resolutions 108 and 109 ratify agreements with two units of the United Public Service Employees Union (UPSEU): City Hall administrative staff and the fire chiefs. Campbell-Cohen asked who bargained for the city. The city's negotiators were Morrissey, Donnelly, and Mahoney, Morrissey said. Campbell-Cohen pointed out that Mahoney's own title is being moved into the union Mahoney negotiated with. Morrissey said the job is supervisory, like others in that unit.

Ordinance 49, amending the 2026 General Fund budget, and Resolution 104, accepting donated Tractor Supply gift cards for the city's animal holding facility, passed without debate.

Budget Review: The Council's Proposed Amendments

Budget review session, Council President Steele presiding. The City Charter requires this session before the council adopts the budget, so both sides can go over the council's proposed changes. The council sent its committee reports and list of amendments earlier that day, and is scheduled to act on the budget on December 1.

Steele opened by hoping the session would run "in the spirit of the resolution we just passed." By Donnelly's count, the council's changes add more than $600,000 in spending and leave about $100,000 still to be balanced. Steele said the council would discuss the feedback.

Council proposalWhat was said
Deputy city engineer ($105,000) moved to salary savingsCampbell-Cohen, who chairs Public Utilities, said the council wants the job filled and would amend the budget once the city hires, the same rule the administration applies to other vacancies. "I want this position filled," he said, and asked for the job posting. Donnelly said removing the money effectively defunds it.
Evening and weekend parking enforcement ($50,000)Struber, who proposed it, said it's for cars blocking crosswalks and driveways or parking illegally along River Street, complaints residents bring to the council, and that the city has no enforcement at night or on weekends. Donnelly warned against ticketing visitors at night and said part-time staff hours can shift without new money.
eCode update ($10,000)Steele said zoning ordinances adopted years ago were never added to the city's online code. The vendor's latest quote is about $9,364, the clerk's office said.
Police vehicles ($250,000) moved to the operating budgetMcKee wants police cars bought as a recurring operating cost rather than through the capital plan. Donnelly said the city would never borrow for vehicles and doesn't consider them a recurring cost.
One deferred traffic job restoredDonnelly said the crew has managed with two people and contractors do almost all signal and light repairs. "We've heard otherwise," Steele said. "We will agree to disagree once again."
Raises for the part-time auditor and clerk's office staffA council member gave the figures: the auditor to $25,000 (up $5,879), the city clerk to $80,000 (up $4,913), the deputy city clerk to $57,921 (up $10,000), and the assistant clerk to $48,391 (up $10,000). Donnelly objected to raises of 17 to 24 percent on top of a budgeted 3 percent. Steele noted the administration had agreed earlier that these jobs were badly underpaid.
Director of Equity and Inclusion Kevin Prior, up $5,000 to $71,306For taking on youth services after the Youth Bureau's specialist job was cut.
Facilities utilities ($400,000) cutStruber said the administration had told the council National Grid credits cover these costs. Mazzariello said the line pays the city's solar contracts, $388,000 so far this year, which generate the credits.
Planning training and dues restoredDiLorenzo said the department is down two people and printing costs are up.

Donnelly also objected to the council's $50,000 part-time attorney. Steele said that pay came from the mayor's own budget and that the council doesn't relitigate the administration's appointments.

Public Forum

Greg Lyons of Second Street, District 4, spoke against Resolution 106 as written and read examples Lyons said show the administration breaking its own pledge. Among them, Lyons cited the mayor's July 31 statement calling council members "too lazy" to review Flock camera data, an August 24 court filing that Lyons said called residents who opposed Flock "paranoid souls," and Donnelly calling the council auditor's October 15 questions "a political charade." Lyons said the city's responses to Lyons' Freedom of Information Law (FOIL) requests contained 24 errors or gaps, and that Lyons and dozens of others have been blocked from the mayor's official Facebook page. Lyons asked the council to amend the resolution so it applies to the administration too.

In the open forum, Frankie, a Troy resident, said they first assumed the council wrote Resolution 106, "given the history of disrespect and emotional tantrums you've had to endure from this administration," and thanked Struber for the amendments. "Let the record show that there is no one from the administration in the room," Frankie said.

Noelle Dommasch of Oakwood Avenue, District 2, said they have asked the administration for basic dignity for two years. Dommasch also told Steele the public will keep reacting out loud in the chamber: "You work for me as public servants, as does the administration."
