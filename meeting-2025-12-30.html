Finance & Special Meeting

December 30, 2025 — Troy City Council

▶ Watch on YouTube Finance Meeting Minutes Special Meeting Minutes

Synopsis

The outgoing Troy City Council held its final legislative session on December 30, 2025, just hours after the incoming Democratic supermajority took the oath of office at a separate ceremony that afternoon. The Finance Meeting opened at 6:00 PM and adjourned at 7:46 PM; the Special Meeting ran from 8:00 PM to 8:17 PM. All seven outgoing members were present throughout.

The session was defined by two controversies. The first: Council Member Brosnan moved a floor amendment to remove Phil's Automotive from the city's towing contract after a constituent described being towed ten minutes before the alternate-side deadline and charged $500 at a cash-only lot. The amendment passed and the council removed Phil's. The second, larger controversy: a $6 million bond for a Frear Park multi-purpose pavilion project passed 5-2, despite Vera and Steele objecting that the council had not received a site plan, project documentation, or a SEQR analysis with adequate notice. Casey and the departing majority framed the vote as enabling park revitalization; Vera and Steele argued it was fiscally irresponsible and procedurally rushed.

The Special Meeting's public forum drew three speakers who challenged both the Frear Park vote and the Casella waste contract. One speaker noted that a resident had called the city clerk at 3:00 PM the same day and been told the SEQR project plans were not available, a potential violation of the 24-hour public availability requirement. This allegation became a recurring issue when the new council took office in January.

The Frear Park bond became a live controversy throughout the new council's first months. At the January 22, 2026 committee night, Council President Steele formally withdrew a rescission resolution after learning the bond sale was already underway, but stated on the record that the administration had made misrepresentations while the rescission was pending.

Key Discussion & Public Testimony

Frear Park Bond (Resolutions 124 & 125)

The administration presented a five-element Frear Park project: a prefabricated restaurant/community building (~150 seats) to replace the long-closed Park Pub; an all-inclusive playground at North Lake; a playground upgrade near the ice rink; new tennis and pickleball courts; and parking expansion. Total project capacity combining the $6M bond with $3M in remaining ARPA funds was estimated at $7–9 million. The project had evolved from a $17M Siemens-designed proposal that was rejected as too costly.

Council Member Casey argued in a floor statement that November's election results, which installed a new Democratic supermajority, represented a mandate, and called the meeting "unnecessarily called." The administration's response: a new council in its first month would face a steep learning curve, and end-of-year bond actions were consistent with prior practice. Council President Steele pressed hard on the absence of a site plan; CHA's representative acknowledged only a rough order-of-magnitude cost estimate existed.

Towing Contract: Resolution 122

Council Member Brosnan described a constituent in his North Central district who was towed at 11:50 AM, ten minutes before the noon alternate-side deadline, then taken to a cash-only lot and charged $500. He moved to amend the resolution removing Phil's Automotive from the approved vendor list. Discussion covered the city's towing fee schedule ($125 base plus $25 per day after 24 hours), the credit card acceptance requirement, and the online dispute process.

Public Forum Speakers

Farrah Fiacco (North Central/Lansingburgh) proposed two improvements to the parking dispute process: separating the online ticket dispute form from the payment form, and requiring towing vendors to post QR codes linking to the dispute process. She also reiterated a virtual parking map proposal she had previously raised with the administration.

Francis Sweet (South Troy) called the decision to bring major items at year-end with incomplete materials "a load of crap," and questioned whether the employees responsible for years of Colony Road landfill dumping were still on the city payroll.

Frankie (Troy), an autistic adult, said that most autistic people she knows would be "astounded" that environmental clearance was being granted without a site plan. She criticized the council's prior pattern of approving bonds without documentation and expressed confidence that the incoming council would "take their jobs seriously." She added: "if you don't ask the public what we would prioritize, then you'll never know. You'll just do whatever you want, just like you did with Proctors."
