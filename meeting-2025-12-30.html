Finance & Special Meeting

December 30, 2025 — Troy City Council

Synopsis

The outgoing Troy City Council held its final legislative session on December 30, 2025 — the same day the incoming Democratic supermajority was sworn in. The Finance Meeting opened at 6:00 PM and adjourned at 7:46 PM; the Special Meeting ran from 8:00 PM to 8:17 PM. All seven outgoing members were present throughout.

The session turned on two items. The first: Council Member Brosnan moved a floor amendment to remove Phil's Automotive from the city's towing contract after a constituent described being towed ten minutes before the alternate-side deadline and charged $500 at a cash-only lot with no recourse. The amendment passed. The second: a $6 million bond for a Frear Park multi-purpose pavilion project passed 5-2. Vera and Steele objected that the council had not received a site plan, project documentation, or a SEQR analysis with adequate advance notice. The administration's own consultant acknowledged that only a rough order-of-magnitude estimate existed.

The Special Meeting's public forum drew three speakers. A resident documented that she had called the city clerk at 3:00 PM that day and been told the SEQR project plans were not available — a potential violation of the 24-hour public availability requirement. She had a recording of the call. This procedural question recurred when the new council took office in January.

The Frear Park bond continued to affect the new council's early months. At the January 22, 2026 committee night, Council President Steele withdrew a rescission resolution after the administration completed the bond sale while the rescission was still pending and did not provide access to bond counsel. Steele stated on the record that "the misrepresentations remain." Campbell-Cohen documented that the SEQR estoppel notice had been signed under the prior city clerk's name after the new clerk had already taken over, and that the environmental determination used an incorrect project classification.

Key Discussion & Public Testimony

Frear Park Bond (Resolutions 124 & 125)

The administration presented a five-element Frear Park project: a prefabricated restaurant/community building (~150 seats), playgrounds, tennis and pickleball courts, and parking expansion, with a total budget estimated at $7–9 million combining the $6M bond with $3M in remaining ARPA funds. No site plan existed; CHA's representative could only offer a rough order-of-magnitude cost estimate. Council President Steele pressed on the absence of documentation; the administration argued that year-end bond actions were consistent with prior practice and that the incoming council would have time to get up to speed.

The bond passed 5-2 over Vera and Steele's objections. As residents later testified at the January 8 public forum, Frear Park neighbors had never been consulted about a pavilion — they had asked for playgrounds.

Towing Contract: Resolution 122

Council Member Brosnan described a constituent in his North Central district who was towed at 11:50 AM — ten minutes before the noon alternate-side deadline — then taken to a cash-only lot and charged $500, with no dispute process available on-site. Brosnan moved a floor amendment removing Phil's Automotive from the approved vendor list; it passed. Discussion covered the city's towing fee schedule ($125 base plus $25 per day after 24 hours), Phil's failure to accept credit cards as required, and the online dispute process that most residents did not know existed.

Public Forum Speakers

Farrah Fiacco (North Central/Lansingburgh) proposed two improvements to the parking dispute process: separating the online ticket dispute form from the payment form, and requiring towing vendors to post QR codes linking to the dispute process. She also reiterated a virtual parking map proposal she had previously raised with the administration.

Francis Sweet (South Troy) called the decision to bring major items at year-end with incomplete materials "a load of crap," and questioned whether the employees responsible for years of Colony Road landfill dumping were still on the city payroll.

Frankie (Troy), an autistic adult, said that most autistic people she knows would be "astounded" that environmental clearance was being granted without a site plan. She criticized the council's prior pattern of approving bonds without documentation and expressed confidence that the incoming council would "take their jobs seriously." She added: "if you don't ask the public what we would prioritize, then you'll never know. You'll just do whatever you want, just like you did with Proctors."
