Synopsis

Three separate Law Committee discussions on April 23 exposed the same pattern: the administration failing to respond to good-faith counterparty outreach over extended periods. CSEA had gone nearly four months without a response to a draft contract. The Nick Board reported the city was $25,000 in arrears on the Knickerbocker Park lease. And the city's own FOIL officer had never submitted an annual compliance report to the council, as David Banks of the Troy Area Labor Council noted in his public forum remarks.

The Law Committee convened earlier in the evening; the Finance Committee called to order at 8:38 PM and adjourned at 9:44 PM with approximately 15 members of the public present. All seven council members were in attendance.

The Finance Committee passed 15 items unanimously, including a $200,000 Lansingburgh Main Street grant administered through TAP, a DEC Urban & Community Forestry grant, a $50,000 EFC engineering planning grant for the water treatment plant, and the CDM Smith sludge lagoon engineering contract. Seven residents testified during an unscheduled public forum, all focused on the mayor's Flock camera emergency declaration and the state of emergency's legal basis.

Law Committee Highlights

CSEA Contract Dispute

CSEA Labor Relations Specialist Jordan Lee and union president Bill Wangler testified that a draft integrated contract was delivered to the city in November 2025; the city failed to respond for nearly four months despite repeated follow-up emails. When the city finally responded in March 2026, it proposed reading the contract "word-for-word, line-by-line," a process Lee said every labor relations specialist he consulted was "stunned by." CSEA has since filed an improper practice charge with PERB. The administration's position: it cannot advance negotiations while a charge is pending. The committee directed the parties to continue communicating; no resolution was reached.

Knickerbocker Park: Nick Board Lease

The Nick Board, the private nonprofit trust that has owned Knickerbocker Park since 1936, testified that the city has been in arrears since December 2025 (approximately $25,000 in unpaid obligations). The dispute centers on a conflict between a 2020 term offer sheet (setting payments at $5,000/month) and the underlying 2007 lease (which runs through 2037 at $1/year). The city's current position is that the 2020 document expired. A DASNY grant of approximately $6 million is funding pool construction at the park; the Nick Board raised concerns about construction plans and insurance coverage during active construction. Mayor Mantello had a heated exchange with council members over the city's obligations.

FOIL Policy Review

Communications Director Alex Horton (the city's FOIL officer) and Corporation Counsel Morrissey testified. Horton estimated spending 1–2 hours per day on FOIL work; she has received in-house training once and has not provided any annual FOIL compliance report to the council. Morrissey stated municipalities are not legally bound by opinions of the NY State Committee on Open Government. Council members pushed for periodic FOIL compliance reports at future meetings.
