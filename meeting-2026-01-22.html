Committee Night & Finance Meeting

January 22, 2026 — Troy City Council

▶ Watch on YouTube Finance Meeting Minutes

Synopsis

Council President Steele withdrew a resolution to rescind the $6 million Frear Park bond at the January 22 Committee Night, after learning the bond sale was already underway. Steele stated that "the misrepresentations remain" and committed to ensuring the council would have direct access to bond counsel going forward. Council Members Campbell-Cohen and McKee identified specific procedural deficiencies in the bond resolution and expressed ongoing concern about the administration's pattern of bypassing council oversight.

The January 15, 2026 NY State Police raids in South Troy also drew significant public testimony, with direct eyewitnesses disputing the police department's characterization of the operation as proportionate and professional. The session ran from approximately 5:35 PM to 8:22 PM, with four committees meeting in sequence: Science & Technology, Law, Public Safety, and Finance. Council Member DiLorenzo was absent due to a death in the family; six of seven members were present throughout.

Three items threaded across multiple committees: the BESS moratorium, the Colonie tactical team intermunicipal agreement, and the Hassan Muradi settlement.

The Finance Committee adjourned at 8:22 PM, with all six votes passing unanimously. A post-agenda forum featured Troy Fire UFA Vice President Matthew Ryan clarifying that the union's concern about BESS was not training but resource capacity: Troy deploys 18 firefighters on an initial alarm while New York City deploys 40 to 50.

Committee Highlights

Science & Technology Committee: BESS Moratorium

Chair Struber convened discussion of Ordinance 3, a proposed six-month moratorium on battery energy storage systems. Mayor Mantello, Fire Chief Saluchi, code officials, and planning staff attended. Key points: approximately 500 BESS facilities operate in New York State, with three fires statewide (Warwick had the most notable incidents) and zero in New York City since 2018. Troy's zoning framework lacks BESS setback guidelines or permit procedures. Struber negotiated a requirement for two-month progress check-ins, later formalized as the key amendment at the Finance Committee vote.

Paul Rogers, a former FDNY hazardous materials expert who helped write NYC's BESS fire code, testified that NYC has had zero BESS incidents since 2018 and that EPRI data shows a 98% decrease in global BESS failures since updated codes were adopted. Rogers argued a moratorium was not necessary given existing state safety requirements, while Troy's Fire UFA president supported the pause, citing resource gaps, not a training deficit.

Public Safety Committee: January 15 Raids

Chair McKee convened discussion on the Colonie Tactical Team IMA and heard testimony about the January 15 NY State Police raids at two South Troy addresses. Troy's Emergency Response Team was tasked with the Fourth Street location; three arrests were made across the operation.

Two direct neighbors to the targeted household, Kendra Lord and Cressa, described waking at 5:00 AM to flashbang explosions, seeing approximately 20 militarized officers, an armored vehicle with an officer mounted on top, a drone, and dogs with night-vision goggles. The household, a mother and three young children, was removed at gunpoint with hands zip-tied. No arrests were made at that location; the family was held in their vehicle for hours then released. They disputed the chief's characterization as proportionate: "officers were laughing after pulling the family out." McKee suggested post-incident community outreach, such as door flyers or neighborhood meetings, to address fear in affected blocks.

Frear Park Bond: Rescission Withdrawn

Council President Steele reported that she had considered calling a special meeting to rescind the $6 million bond but learned the bond sale was already underway and rescission was not in the city's best interests. The rescission resolution was withdrawn. Steele stated that "the misrepresentations remain." Specifically, the bond could only be applied to a restaurant, not the broader park amenities that were pitched to the council. She had been stonewalled for weeks when requesting access to bond counsel. Campbell-Cohen identified two technical deficiencies: the estoppel notice was signed under the prior city clerk's name after the new clerk had taken over, and the SEQR determination listed the project as "unclassified" when state agency officials confirmed it was not accurate.
